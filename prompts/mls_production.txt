MYESTATEPICS MLS INTERIOR — PRODUCTION PROMPT V4.0.7

PHOTO CORRECTION ONLY

The original photograph is the absolute source of truth. Create a professional
MLS interior enhancement that looks like a carefully edited Lightroom image.

Do not redesign, restage, add, remove, replace, move, or reshape anything.
Do not alter architecture, furniture, fixtures, room geometry, window geometry,
reflections, or exterior structures.

PRIORITY ORDER

1. Preserve exact architecture, materials, and geometry.
2. For every genuine exterior-facing window or glass door, perform the
   mandatory professional MLS window pull defined below.
3. Keep the interior bright and natural with useful shadow and midtone detail.
4. Correct only artificial low-frequency illumination inconsistency on large
   continuous surfaces while retaining real light falloff and room depth.
5. Preserve a natural photographic look; secondary cosmetic correction never
   weakens items 1–4.

MATERIAL COLOR LOCK

Preserve the photographed hue and relative color of walls, ceilings, trim,
cabinets, countertops, flooring, tile, stone, wood, carpet, rugs, furniture,
fabric, and fixtures.

Beige remains beige. Greige remains greige. Cream remains cream. Warm wood
remains its photographed warm wood—not orange. Dark cabinets remain their
photographed color. Do not turn gray into beige, beige into yellow, brown wood
into orange, or neutral hardwood into saturated warm wood. Do not whiten, gray,
cool, warm, enrich, or desaturate materials.

STRICTLY NEUTRAL WHITE BALANCE

Neutral walls, ceilings, trim, white cabinetry, and white countertops must
render neutral—not yellow, orange, green, magenta, blue, cyan, or cold gray.

Remove any unwanted global color cast, but do not replace one cast with
another. Preserve genuinely warm bulbs and warm materials locally without
allowing that warmth to spread across the whole room.

EXPOSURE AND DEPTH

Target a bright, natural, consistent MLS interior across rooms and angles.
Brighten only as much as needed using balanced exposure, strong shadow recovery,
and midtone recovery while preserving highlights, natural blacks, and
architectural depth. Avoid global overexposure, glowing walls, clipped ceilings,
gray shadows, flat HDR tonality, haze, or synthetic rendering.

WINDOW FIDELITY — AUTHORITATIVE

EVERY GENUINE EXTERIOR-FACING WINDOW OR GLASS DOOR IS A MANDATORY CORRECTION
REGION. Perform a strong professional MLS window pull. Recover the MAXIMUM
REALISTIC exterior information visible through the original window. The outdoor
view must be immediately readable, crisp, clear, and naturally contrasted—not
faint, hazy, milky, foggy, washed out, nearly white, or barely visible.

Where present in the source, clearly reveal genuine trees, landscaping, lawn,
neighboring houses, buildings, roads, fences, exterior structures, ground, and
natural outdoor detail. A viewer must immediately understand what is outside.
Partial recovery is not sufficient: a white window, white sky, washed-out
exterior, hazy exterior, faint trees, or barely visible landscaping is a failed
MLS window pull.

Never create, infer, or introduce a new window, opening, house, neighboring
building, roof, garage, fence, vehicle, tree, road, structure, or exterior
architectural feature whose existence is not supported by the source image.
Never convert an ordinary wall, door, opening, bright region, mirror, shower
glass, reflection, cabinet glass, glossy surface, screen, or appliance into a
window. Never invent exterior architecture merely to make a window pull look
complete.

Preserve all source-supported exterior objects, including houses, trees,
landscaping, decks, fences, roads, driveways, vehicles, roofs, ground, horizon,
and window frames. Do not replace or invent houses, buildings, trees,
landscaping, lawn, roads, fences, ground, horizon, exterior architecture, or a
plausible different outdoor scene.

Through sheer curtains, blinds, screens, or similar material, preserve the
natural physical softness and partial obscuration. Do not create unnaturally
crisp exterior detail or reconstruct hidden buildings or objects.

When fallback sky is viewed through blinds, sheer or translucent curtains,
screens, or partially closed coverings, apply extra optical restraint: reduce
sky saturation and contrast, use minimal or no cloud definition, retain the
actual covering texture and boundaries, and do not make the covering
artificially transparent. Exterior detail must remain believable through the
obstruction, not vivid, crisp, or high-contrast behind it. If daytime sky is
visible through or between the covering, it must still visibly read as light
natural blue, not white, gray, or colorless.

First recover and preserve genuine sky information from the original
photograph. Correct its exposure, highlight-clipping appearance, haze, and
excessive whiteness without replacing recoverable real sky. Whenever daytime
sky is visibly present through a real window, it must read as recognizably
light natural MLS blue at normal MLS viewing size. Restrained never means white,
near-white, gray-white, colorless, or barely perceptible blue that visually
reads as white; each of those is a failed sky result.

If and only if visible sky pixels alone are genuinely clipped and contain no
recoverable sky information, replace only those irrecoverably clipped sky
pixels with a light natural MLS-blue fallback. It must be clearly blue enough
to read as daytime sky while remaining moderate-to-low saturation,
moderate-to-low contrast, photorealistic, daylight-consistent, and visually
subordinate to the property, trees, buildings, and landscape. This approved
fallback does not permit a white sky to remain.

Do not generate dramatic or clearly defined fallback clouds. If cloud texture
is needed, it must be extremely soft, faint, low contrast, naturally
integrated, and barely noticeable; a clean light natural-blue sky is preferable
to an obvious synthetic cloudscape.

Apply blue only to actual sky pixels; never tint glass as a whole, buildings,
houses, trees, roofs, decks, fences, landscaping, lawn, ground, horizon, window
frames, curtains, blinds, reflections, or interior surfaces. Never use sky
fallback to invent or alter exterior architecture, objects, horizon geometry,
or scenery. Avoid deep blue, royal blue, electric blue, cobalt blue, cyan-heavy
blue, turquoise, oversaturated blue, dramatic, cinematic, HDR-like,
attention-grabbing, stylized, postcard-like, or artificial AI-looking sky.
Preserve clean window-frame, mullion, blind, curtain, glass, wall, and exterior
geometry with no halos, glowing frames, blue bleed, fake masking edges, or
manufactured visibility through opaque material.

Never create a fake window, fake outdoor scenery, or fake reflection. Mirrors
must continue to reflect the original room; never generate outdoor scenery
inside a mirror, reflection, shower glass, cabinet glass, glossy surface,
screen, or appliance.

OUTPUT QUALITY

Retain natural texture, fine detail, and photographic realism. Avoid painterly,
rendered, plastic, AI-generated, HDR-heavy, hazy, or overprocessed appearance.
Do not add text, logos, watermarks, borders, or artificial framing.

ARCHITECTURAL FIDELITY

The original photograph is the absolute source of truth.

Never create, remove, enlarge, shrink, move, or reinterpret any architectural feature.

This includes:

- windows
- doors
- mirrors
- reflections
- walls
- ceilings
- floors
- openings
- railings
- staircases
- cabinets
- fireplaces
- trim
- built-in fixtures

Never invent architecture that does not exist.

Mirror reflections must remain physically accurate to the original photograph.

If an area is ambiguous, preserve the original rather than reconstructing it.

Architectural fidelity always has higher priority than brightness, aesthetics, or window enhancement.

HARDWOOD FLOOR CONTINUITY

Treat one continuous hardwood floor as one photographed material. Preserve its
exact original wood color, grain, plank pattern, plank geometry, directional
light, natural tonal variation, legitimate soft reflections, and natural sheen.
Correct only localized excessive window-driven glare: blown reflective streaks,
white or yellow floor flare, harsh exposure bands, distracting bright strips,
isolated hotspots, and artificial exposure discontinuities that make the same
continuous hardwood substantially brighter than surrounding floor.

Compress excessive highlight intensity toward the underlying hardwood color
family while retaining believable directional daylight and the general footprint
of legitimate soft reflection. Do not remove all reflection, flatten the whole
floor, globally darken the room, create a local patch, smear texture, repeat
grain, or leave visible mask boundaries. Transitions must be broad, smooth,
gradual, and physically believable. Never recolor, replace, invent grain,
erase real grain, alter plank geometry, turn dark brown light brown, brown
orange/yellow, dark wood gray, or natural wood into synthetic brown.

WALL AND CEILING CONTINUITY

Preserve exact original paint color, material, architecture, natural light
falloff, corners, real shadows, trim, molding, outlets, switches, vents,
fixtures, doors, and openings. Correct only artificial localized highlight,
darkness, white-balance drift, color contamination, gray patches, dark
patches, dirty blotches, cloudy spots, faint wavy tonal variation, exposure
bands, or AI-made mottling on otherwise smooth neutral walls, ceilings, and
continuous carpet. Do not repaint, recolor, flatten, plasticize, erase
legitimate shadows, erase features, or manufacture texture.

MIRROR AND PHOTOGRAPHY-EQUIPMENT REFLECTIONS

If a real mirror or clear reflective surface visibly contains a photographer,
camera, tripod, phone, or obvious photography equipment, remove that
photography-related reflection cleanly and reconstruct only the physically
plausible reflected continuation already implied by the source. Do not alter
the faces of legitimate people intentionally present in a property photograph.
Photographer, camera, tripod, or phone remaining in the mirror is a failure.
Do not change mirror size, position, frame, reflected room geometry, doors,
windows, walls, furniture, architecture, or legitimate reflected objects.

LOCAL MATERIAL HIGHLIGHT PROTECTION

Keep the successful overall MLS exposure, shadow recovery, window pull, and
room brightness. Do not globally darken the photograph to correct a local
material highlight.

For continuous hardwood flooring in direct sunlight or other bright areas,
preserve the real directional light and natural transition into shade. Retain
wood grain, original wood color, saturation, plank geometry, and legitimate
reflections inside the bright area. Correct only excessive local highlight
clipping, washout, or desaturation that makes the same continuous floor look
unnaturally pale or inconsistent. Never remove legitimate sunlight, flatten
the floor, recolor wood, oversaturate wood, or manufacture grain.

For a continuous painted wall or ceiling, preserve its original paint color,
subtle natural illumination gradient, corners, shadows, and material
appearance. Correct only local over-brightening, washout, color drift, or
flatness that makes one area of the same surface look like different paint.
Never globally darken the room, repaint the surface, erase legitimate light
falloff, or make the surface flat or plastic.

Do not add clarity, sharpening, microcontrast, texture enhancement, halos, or
crosshatch artifacts to solve these local material-highlight issues. Smooth
surfaces must remain smooth; wood grain must remain crisp and natural.
