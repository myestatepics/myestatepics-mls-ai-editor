MYESTATEPICS MLS INTERIOR — PRODUCTION BASE PROMPT v1.6

PHOTO CORRECTION ONLY

The original photograph is the absolute source of truth. Create a professional
MLS interior enhancement that looks like a carefully edited Lightroom image.

Do not redesign, restage, add, remove, replace, move, or reshape anything.
Do not alter architecture, furniture, fixtures, room geometry, window geometry,
reflections, or exterior structures.

PRIORITY ORDER

1. Preserve exact material identity and room geometry.
2. Establish strictly neutral white balance with no global cast.
3. Produce naturally bright MLS exposure while retaining depth.
4. Recover crisp, realistic exterior detail through windows.
5. Make genuine visible open sky soft natural light blue.

MATERIAL COLOR LOCK

Preserve the photographed hue and relative color of walls, ceilings, trim,
cabinets, countertops, flooring, tile, stone, wood, carpet, rugs, furniture,
fabric, and fixtures.

Beige remains beige. Greige remains greige. Cream remains cream. Warm wood
remains warm wood. Dark cabinets remain their photographed color. Do not
whiten, gray, cool, warm, enrich, or desaturate materials.

STRICTLY NEUTRAL WHITE BALANCE

Neutral walls, ceilings, trim, white cabinetry, and white countertops must
render neutral—not yellow, orange, green, magenta, blue, cyan, or cold gray.

Remove any unwanted global color cast, but do not replace one cast with
another. Preserve genuinely warm bulbs and warm materials locally without
allowing that warmth to spread across the whole room.

EXPOSURE AND DEPTH

Brighten only as much as needed for a professional MLS result. Use balanced
exposure, shadow recovery, and midtone recovery. Preserve black depth and
architectural dimensionality. Avoid glowing walls, clipped ceilings, gray
shadows, flat HDR tonality, haze, or synthetic rendering.

WINDOW DETAIL

Recover real exterior detail present in the source: brick, siding, roofs,
window frames, trees, grass, landscaping, and neighboring structures.

Exterior detail should be crisp, clear, and naturally contrasted—not soft,
foggy, washed out, or covered by cyan haze. Preserve clean edges without
halos or artificial oversharpening.

LIGHT-BLUE SKY THROUGH WINDOWS

Where genuine open sky is visible through a window, that open-sky area must
appear as a subtle, natural, light daytime blue.

Apply blue only to genuine open-sky pixels. Never tint window glass as a whole,
reflections, buildings, roofs, siding, brick, trees, grass, landscaping,
window frames, curtains, walls, ceilings, cabinetry, countertops, or furniture.

If uncertain whether an area is sky or structure, preserve the photographed
color and do not apply blue. Do not invent buildings, trees, landscaping,
clouds, or other exterior scenery.

OUTPUT QUALITY

Retain natural texture, fine detail, and photographic realism. Avoid painterly,
rendered, plastic, AI-generated, HDR-heavy, hazy, or overprocessed appearance.
Do not add text, logos, watermarks, borders, or artificial framing.

ARCHITECTURAL FIDELITY

The original photograph is the absolute source of truth.

Never create, remove, enlarge, shrink, move, or reinterpret any architectural feature.

This includes:

- windows
- doors
- mirrors
- reflections
- walls
- ceilings
- floors
- openings
- railings
- staircases
- cabinets
- fireplaces
- trim
- built-in fixtures

Never invent architecture that does not exist.

Never create windows inside mirror reflections.

Never transform blank walls or bright regions into windows or openings.

Mirror reflections must remain physically accurate to the original photograph.

If an area is ambiguous, preserve the original rather than reconstructing it.

Architectural fidelity always has higher priority than brightness, aesthetics, or window enhancement.

STRUCTURAL LOCK — HIGHEST PRIORITY

Preserve the original architecture exactly. Never create, insert, remove,
enlarge, shrink, relocate, or modify windows, doors, skylights, wall openings,
arches, or structural openings.

If there is no window in the original photograph, do not create one. Walls must
remain walls. Only edit windows that already exist in the original photograph.

Treat a region as a real exterior window only when the source photograph shows
all three: a physical window frame, an exterior wall boundary, and a visible
outside scene.

Never treat mirrors, shower glass, reflections, cabinet glass, glossy tile,
polished stone, TV screens, door gaps, open interior doors, bright highlights,
chrome, or appliances as windows.

Never create a window. Never create blue sky unless editing an existing,
confirmed exterior window. If uncertain whether a region is a real exterior
window, leave that region unchanged.

WINDOW PULL

For an existing confirmed window, perform a strong, natural MLS-quality window pull.
Recover the real exterior whenever possible.

Preserve every real exterior object from the original photograph, including
neighboring houses, trees, landscaping, decks, fences, roads, driveways,
vehicles, roofs, and all other outdoor objects.

Do not replace the complete exterior view. Do not invent a different exterior
or substitute any outdoor object.

SKY REPLACEMENT — EXISTING WINDOWS ONLY

When the original sky region is blown out, white, gray, or unattractive,
replace only that same sky portion with a clean, natural light-blue MLS sky.
Apply blue only to pixels belonging to the actual exterior sky region.

Keep every real exterior object unchanged. Do not allow blue to bleed onto
houses, trees, roofs, decks, fences, landscaping, window frames, curtains,
blinds, or any other non-sky surface.

MIRRORS AND REFLECTIONS

Never create a fake window, fake outdoor scenery, or fake reflection. Never
generate outdoor scenery inside a mirror, reflection, shower glass, cabinet
glass, glossy surface, screen, or appliance.

Mirrors must continue to reflect the original room. Preserve mirrors,
reflections, glass, window frames, blinds, curtains, and all architectural
details exactly as photographed.

PRESERVE EVERYTHING ELSE

Do not change room geometry, camera position, perspective, materials, wall
colors, floor colors, cabinet colors, countertops, trim, doors, windows, or
furniture placement.

The objective is to improve the photograph, not redesign it. If uncertain
whether an area is genuine exterior sky or another surface, preserve the
photographed region unchanged.

HARDWOOD FLOOR CONTINUITY

Treat one continuous hardwood floor as one photographed material. Preserve its
exact original wood color, grain, plank pattern, plank geometry, directional
light, and legitimate reflections. Correct only localized excessive highlight,
washed-out exposure, color contamination, or white-balance inconsistency so
the same floor reads naturally as one continuous surface. Never recolor,
replace, flatten, invent grain, erase real grain, or alter plank geometry.

WALL AND CEILING CONTINUITY

Preserve exact original paint color, material, architecture, natural light
falloff, corners, real shadows, trim, molding, outlets, switches, vents,
fixtures, doors, and openings. Correct only artificial localized highlight,
darkness, white-balance drift, color contamination, gray patches, dark
patches, dirty blotches, cloudy spots, faint wavy tonal variation, or AI-made
mottling on otherwise smooth neutral walls and ceilings. Do not repaint,
recolor, flatten, plasticize, erase legitimate shadows, erase features, or
manufacture texture.

MIRROR AND PHOTOGRAPHY-EQUIPMENT REFLECTIONS

If a real mirror or clear reflective surface visibly contains only a
photographer, camera, tripod, phone, or obvious photography equipment,
remove that photography-related reflection cleanly and reconstruct only the
physically plausible reflected continuation already implied by the source.
Do not change mirror size, position, frame, reflected room geometry, doors,
windows, walls, furniture, architecture, or legitimate reflected objects.

LOCAL MATERIAL HIGHLIGHT PROTECTION

Keep the successful overall MLS exposure, shadow recovery, window pull, and
room brightness. Do not globally darken the photograph to correct a local
material highlight.

For continuous hardwood flooring in direct sunlight or other bright areas,
preserve the real directional light and natural transition into shade. Retain
wood grain, original wood color, saturation, plank geometry, and legitimate
reflections inside the bright area. Correct only excessive local highlight
clipping, washout, or desaturation that makes the same continuous floor look
unnaturally pale or inconsistent. Never remove legitimate sunlight, flatten
the floor, recolor wood, oversaturate wood, or manufacture grain.

For a continuous painted wall or ceiling, preserve its original paint color,
subtle natural illumination gradient, corners, shadows, and material
appearance. Correct only local over-brightening, washout, color drift, or
flatness that makes one area of the same surface look like different paint.
Never globally darken the room, repaint the surface, erase legitimate light
falloff, or make the surface flat or plastic.

Do not add clarity, sharpening, microcontrast, texture enhancement, halos, or
crosshatch artifacts to solve these local material-highlight issues. Smooth
surfaces must remain smooth; wood grain must remain crisp and natural.
