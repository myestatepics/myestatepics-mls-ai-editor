MYESTATEPICS MLS INTERIOR — PRODUCTION PROMPT V4.0.3

PHOTO CORRECTION ONLY

The original photograph is the absolute source of truth. Create a professional
MLS interior enhancement that looks like a carefully edited Lightroom image.

Do not redesign, restage, add, remove, replace, move, or reshape anything.
Do not alter architecture, furniture, fixtures, room geometry, window geometry,
reflections, or exterior structures.

PRIORITY ORDER

1. Deliver a professional MLS window pull through genuine exterior-facing
   windows and glass doors.
2. Preserve exact material identity, color, and room geometry.
3. Produce a bright natural MLS interior with useful shadow and midtone detail.
4. Correct only artificial low-frequency illumination inconsistency on large
   continuous surfaces while retaining real light falloff and depth.
5. Preserve architectural depth and a natural photographic look.
6. Cost selection is external to this prompt and must never weaken items 1–5.

MATERIAL COLOR LOCK

Preserve the photographed hue and relative color of walls, ceilings, trim,
cabinets, countertops, flooring, tile, stone, wood, carpet, rugs, furniture,
fabric, and fixtures.

Beige remains beige. Greige remains greige. Cream remains cream. Warm wood
remains warm wood. Dark cabinets remain their photographed color. Do not
whiten, gray, cool, warm, enrich, or desaturate materials.

STRICTLY NEUTRAL WHITE BALANCE

Neutral walls, ceilings, trim, white cabinetry, and white countertops must
render neutral—not yellow, orange, green, magenta, blue, cyan, or cold gray.

Remove any unwanted global color cast, but do not replace one cast with
another. Preserve genuinely warm bulbs and warm materials locally without
allowing that warmth to spread across the whole room.

EXPOSURE AND DEPTH

Target a bright, natural, consistent MLS interior across rooms and angles.
Brighten only as much as needed using balanced exposure, strong shadow recovery,
and midtone recovery while preserving highlights, natural blacks, and
architectural depth. Avoid global overexposure, glowing walls, clipped ceilings,
gray shadows, flat HDR tonality, haze, or synthetic rendering.

WINDOW FIDELITY — AUTHORITATIVE

STRONG PROFESSIONAL MLS WINDOW PULL IS REQUIRED for a real existing
exterior-facing window or glass door. Recover the real exterior first: deliver
clearly readable source-supported trees, landscaping, houses, buildings,
ground, roads, fences, and structures with realistic photographic contrast,
while preserving the identity and geometry of the actual exterior scene.

Never create, infer, or introduce a new window, opening, house, neighboring
building, roof, garage, fence, vehicle, tree, road, structure, or exterior
architectural feature whose existence is not supported by the source image.
Never convert an ordinary wall, door, opening, bright region, mirror, shower
glass, reflection, cabinet glass, glossy surface, screen, or appliance into a
window. Never invent exterior architecture merely to make a window pull look
complete.

Preserve all source-supported exterior objects, including houses, trees,
landscaping, decks, fences, roads, driveways, vehicles, roofs, and window
frames. If exterior architecture or objects are incomplete, clipped, hidden,
blurred, or uncertain, enhance only what is supported by the source image; do
not fabricate a plausible exterior scene.

Through sheer curtains, blinds, screens, or similar material, preserve the
natural physical softness and partial obscuration. Do not create unnaturally
crisp exterior detail or reconstruct hidden buildings or objects.

Where real sky is visibly present through a real window, preserve or recover it
as a light, soft, natural, low-saturation daytime blue with real clouds when
recoverable. If the visible sky portion alone is irrecoverably clipped to
featureless white, replace only that sky portion with the same restrained light
natural MLS blue fallback; featureless white sky is not an acceptable result.
Apply blue only to actual sky pixels; never tint glass as a whole, buildings,
trees, roofs, decks, fences, landscaping, window frames, curtains, blinds,
reflections, or interior surfaces. Never use sky fallback to invent or alter
exterior architecture, objects, horizon geometry, or scenery. Avoid deep blue,
royal blue, electric blue, cobalt blue, cyan-heavy blue, turquoise,
oversaturated blue, dramatic HDR blue, or artificial AI-looking sky. Factual
exterior preservation has priority over aesthetic perfection.

Never create a fake window, fake outdoor scenery, or fake reflection. Mirrors
must continue to reflect the original room; never generate outdoor scenery
inside a mirror, reflection, shower glass, cabinet glass, glossy surface,
screen, or appliance.

OUTPUT QUALITY

Retain natural texture, fine detail, and photographic realism. Avoid painterly,
rendered, plastic, AI-generated, HDR-heavy, hazy, or overprocessed appearance.
Do not add text, logos, watermarks, borders, or artificial framing.

ARCHITECTURAL FIDELITY

The original photograph is the absolute source of truth.

Never create, remove, enlarge, shrink, move, or reinterpret any architectural feature.

This includes:

- windows
- doors
- mirrors
- reflections
- walls
- ceilings
- floors
- openings
- railings
- staircases
- cabinets
- fireplaces
- trim
- built-in fixtures

Never invent architecture that does not exist.

Mirror reflections must remain physically accurate to the original photograph.

If an area is ambiguous, preserve the original rather than reconstructing it.

Architectural fidelity always has higher priority than brightness, aesthetics, or window enhancement.

HARDWOOD FLOOR CONTINUITY

Treat one continuous hardwood floor as one photographed material. Preserve its
exact original wood color, grain, plank pattern, plank geometry, directional
light, and legitimate reflections. Correct only localized excessive highlight,
washed-out exposure, color contamination, or white-balance inconsistency so
the same floor reads naturally as one continuous surface. Never recolor,
replace, flatten, invent grain, erase real grain, or alter plank geometry.

WALL AND CEILING CONTINUITY

Preserve exact original paint color, material, architecture, natural light
falloff, corners, real shadows, trim, molding, outlets, switches, vents,
fixtures, doors, and openings. Correct only artificial localized highlight,
darkness, white-balance drift, color contamination, gray patches, dark
patches, dirty blotches, cloudy spots, faint wavy tonal variation, exposure
bands, or AI-made mottling on otherwise smooth neutral walls, ceilings, and
continuous carpet. Do not repaint, recolor, flatten, plasticize, erase
legitimate shadows, erase features, or manufacture texture.

MIRROR AND PHOTOGRAPHY-EQUIPMENT REFLECTIONS

If a real mirror or clear reflective surface visibly contains only a
photographer, camera, tripod, phone, or obvious photography equipment,
remove that photography-related reflection cleanly and reconstruct only the
physically plausible reflected continuation already implied by the source.
Do not change mirror size, position, frame, reflected room geometry, doors,
windows, walls, furniture, architecture, or legitimate reflected objects.

LOCAL MATERIAL HIGHLIGHT PROTECTION

Keep the successful overall MLS exposure, shadow recovery, window pull, and
room brightness. Do not globally darken the photograph to correct a local
material highlight.

For continuous hardwood flooring in direct sunlight or other bright areas,
preserve the real directional light and natural transition into shade. Retain
wood grain, original wood color, saturation, plank geometry, and legitimate
reflections inside the bright area. Correct only excessive local highlight
clipping, washout, or desaturation that makes the same continuous floor look
unnaturally pale or inconsistent. Never remove legitimate sunlight, flatten
the floor, recolor wood, oversaturate wood, or manufacture grain.

For a continuous painted wall or ceiling, preserve its original paint color,
subtle natural illumination gradient, corners, shadows, and material
appearance. Correct only local over-brightening, washout, color drift, or
flatness that makes one area of the same surface look like different paint.
Never globally darken the room, repaint the surface, erase legitimate light
falloff, or make the surface flat or plastic.

Do not add clarity, sharpening, microcontrast, texture enhancement, halos, or
crosshatch artifacts to solve these local material-highlight issues. Smooth
surfaces must remain smooth; wood grain must remain crisp and natural.
